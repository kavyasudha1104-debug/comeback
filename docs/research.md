# Research

Done on 9–10 November 2026, using my own SuperKalam account, SuperKalam's public pages, reviews and other prep apps. Screenshots are in `screenshots/superkalam/`.

| Screen | What's on it | Screenshot |
|---|---|---|
| My Targets | Four targets: Daily Prelims Practice, Daily Mains Questions, Daily News Analysis, GS Learning | [my-targets](../screenshots/superkalam/my-targets.png) |

| Website Learn page | 18 subjects, 259 units in total. Five tagged MAINS: Governance, Internal Security, Disaster Management, World History, Ethics | (copied into `comeback/catalog.py`) |

When you miss a day, the habits reset and the learning path resumes where you stopped. So there's no pile of missed work, but your finish date quietly moves later.

I also asked SuperKalam's Ask AI how the app handles missed days and targets ([1](../screenshots/superkalam/ask-ai-missed-days.png), [2](../screenshots/superkalam/ask-ai-targets.png)). It found nothing about the app adjusting a personal plan, which matches what I saw.

I couldn't see the paid plan's Weekly Progress Report. The [help page](https://superkalam.com/help-and-support) describes it as showing strengths and weaknesses, not pace.

## What SuperKalam says

- Their blog says one missed day creates a backlog, the backlog causes stress, and many aspirants stop because they feel they can't catch up. ([blog](https://superkalam.com/upsc-preparation/tips/how-to-be-consistent-in-upsc-preparation-tips-by-toppers))
- An article says that if you miss a session, the AI adjusts your future sessions, without saying how. ([article](https://superkalam.com/upsc-preparation/strategy/time-management-app-for-ias-preparation)) A Play Store review says the app "fixes my timetable when I miss a day". ([Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.superkalam&hl=en_IN)) Both fit the path resuming where you stopped.
- The paid FOCUS batch has a fixed timetable with milestones like "this completes the entire Prelims syllabus". That's a pace plan, but for a whole batch, not one person. ([FOCUS](https://superkalam.com/focus-batch-2026-working-professional))

## Other apps already do this

| Product | What it does |
|---|---|
| [Poro](https://poroapp.com/syllabus-tracker/) | Says whether you'll finish each subject at your pace, and how many days late |
| [Lighthouse Prep](https://lighthouseprep.in/study-planner/upsc) | Plans back from the exam date and cuts the routine down when you fall behind |
| [upsc.ai](https://upsc.ai/tools/upsc-progress-tracker) | Adaptive timetable with "auto slip recovery" |
| [Proxy Gyan](https://www.proxygyan.com/upsc-timetable) | Says when you'll finish each subject |
| [SyncStudy](https://www.syncstudy.in/tools/free-study-timetable-generator) | Shifts the plan when you miss a day, keeping the exam deadline |

So the idea isn't new. The gap is that SuperKalam has everything needed to answer "will I finish in time?" (exam year, the 259-unit path, daily progress) but doesn't show it.

## On falling behind

- An aspirant's Medium post, "Reasons why I left UPSC": they couldn't clear a backlog after missing classes and gave up. ([Medium](https://medium.com/being/reasons-why-i-left-upsc-c8c5d0548ac3))
- A UPSC educator suggests striking off a backlog that's become overwhelming rather than chasing it. ([X](https://x.com/krishna_tupe/status/1578997344737013760))
- For missed news, the common advice is one compilation instead of old papers. ([Raj IAS](https://rajiasacademy.com/upsc-current-affairs-how-much-is-enough/), [Plutus IAS](https://plutusias.com/how-many-months-of-current-affairs-are-enough-for-upsc-prelims/))
- A coaching guide recommends a priority order when you fall behind, rather than recovering everything. ([The Prayas India](https://theprayasindia.com/how-to-manage-time-during-upsc-preparation/))
